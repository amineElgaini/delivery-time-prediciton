**How to compare coefficients (once you have a trained Ridge/linear model, in Step 3-4):**

1. **Extract them:**
```python
coefficients = pd.DataFrame({
    'feature': X_train.columns,
    'coefficient': model.coef_
})
coefficients = coefficients.sort_values('coefficient', key=abs, ascending=False)
print(coefficients)
```

2. **Compare within the same group.** `City` becomes `City_Urban` and `City_Semi-Urban` after encoding — compare their coefficients to each other, not to unrelated features.

3. **Look for a suspicious gap.** If `City_Urban = 2.3` but `City_Semi-Urban = 18.4`, that's a red flag — one city type shouldn't logically cause 8x more delay than a similar one.

4. **Check the overall scale.** If most feature coefficients sit around 1-6 minutes, but one jumps to 18+, that's the standout to investigate — likely noise from too little data (141 rows), not a real effect.

Ridge is Linear Regression plus a small penalty. The penalty stops the model from giving any feature a huge weight.
Linear Regression finds the weight for each feature that gives the smallest error. Nothing stops a weight from getting very big.
Ridge does the same, but it also adds a cost for big weights. So it prefers smaller, calmer weights, even if the error gets a tiny bit worse.
-------------------
**Linear Regression vs Ridge**

- **Linear Regression** finds the weight for each feature that gives the smallest error. Nothing stops a weight from becoming very big.
- **Ridge** is the same, plus a small penalty for big weights. It prefers calm, small weights.

**When this matters**
- When two columns carry almost the same information (like `distance_km` and `distance_miles`), Linear Regression can't decide how to share the credit between them.
- It may pick weights like **+50 and -48**. They cancel each other, so the training predictions look fine.

**Why big canceling weights are a problem**
1. **They break on new data.** A tiny difference between the two columns gets multiplied by the big weights, so predictions can jump.
2. **They are unstable.** Retrain on slightly different data and the weights change a lot.
3. **They can't be explained.** "+50 for this, -48 for that" tells the business nothing.

Ridge avoids this by choosing small weights (like +1 and +1) instead.

**In your project**
- You have about 32,000 rows and only 20 features, and your features are not near-copies.
- So the weights were already calm, and Ridge changed almost nothing. You saw it yourself: Age 2.23906 vs 2.23919.
- Result: Linear and Ridge scored almost the same (R² 0.5897). We ignore Ridge from now on.
